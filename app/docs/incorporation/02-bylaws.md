# Bylaws — Holons Coop

A California nonprofit mutual benefit corporation operating as a cooperative.
Adopted by the board of directors on [date].

> Draft for counsel review before adoption. Blanks are completed at adoption.

## Article 1 — Offices and Records

1.1 **Principal office.** The principal office is in the County of Imperial, California, at the
address stated in the articles or at another location the board selects.

1.2 **Records.** The corporation keeps at its principal office, or at a place the board
designates, the articles, bylaws, minutes, accounting records, the membership ledger and the
records required by the California Nonprofit Corporation Law. Members may inspect records as
Article 11 provides and as the law requires.

1.3 **Fiscal year.** The fiscal year begins on 1 January and ends on 31 December.

1.4 **Seal.** The corporation may use a seal, but its absence does not affect the validity of any
instrument.

## Article 2 — Members

2.1 **Eligibility.** Membership is open to natural persons who apply, are approved by the board,
pay the capital contribution and dues in effect, and sign the required agreements.

2.2 **One class, one vote.** There is one class of members. Each member in good standing has one
vote on every matter submitted to the members, regardless of site-license tier.

2.3 **Site-license tiers and occupancy types.** Standard, Preferred and Premium tiers reflect
location, shade, privacy and convenience only. They create no additional vote, no ownership
percentage and no deeded interest. The board may adjust tier pricing and availability. The board may
also offer occupancy types — resident (year-round), seasonal and absent-owner — as terms of the
occupancy agreement. Occupancy type is not a class of membership, creates no additional vote, and
may be changed only as the occupancy agreement and the approved use allow.

2.4 **Capital contributions.** The board sets the contribution for each tier. The contributions
in effect at adoption are:

| Tier | Contribution | Memberships | Total |
|------|--------------|-------------|-------|
| Standard | $7,500 | 8 | $60,000 |
| Preferred | $10,000 | 8 | $80,000 |
| Premium | $12,500 | 4 | $50,000 |
| **Total** | | **20** | **$190,000** |

Each contribution purchases one membership share in the corporation. A share is personal property
held by the member and recorded in the membership ledger; the corporation holds title to the parcel
and improvements for the benefit of the members. A share is not a deed, not a recorded lot and not
an interest in land. Contributions are capital funding for the park, not rent, not a deposit and
not a guaranteed refundable amount. They are held in the corporation's account and applied to
approved project costs.

2.5 **Dues.** Dues are $150 per member per month. Dues support the baseline operating budget: water
and waste service, administration, upkeep and reserves. The board may adjust dues annually with at
least 30 days' written notice, but no single annual increase mayexceed 10% without member approval under Article 10, and dues may not be set below the level needed to meet debt service and
reserve requirements while secured debt is outstanding. Where dues are assigned to a lender under
Article 5.5, the board must bill and collect them as the assignment requires.

2.6 **Capital calls.** The board may propose a capital call for an approved project. A capital call
requires the approval of a majority of members voting, and the board must state the purpose, amount,
timing and consequence of nonpayment.

2.7 **Admission.** The board approves admissions by resolution or by a written procedure it adopts.
An applicant becomes a member when approved, paid and documented in the membership ledger.

2.8 **Resignation and refunds.** A member may resign in writing. A capital contribution is not
refundable on demand. The board may adopt a redemption policy that is subject to the corporation's
reserves, its debts, the approved budget and California law. No refund or redemption is guaranteed.

2.9 **Transfer.** Membership is not transferable without board approval. A proposed transfer must be
in writing, the transferee must qualify for admission, and any transfer fee set by the board must be
paid. The corporation may decline a transfer that would breach the approved occupancy limits or the
offering documents.

2.10 **Termination for cause.** The board may terminate a membership after written notice and an
opportunity to be heard for nonpayment beyond 30 days, breach of the occupancy or guest rules,
conduct that endangers others, or misrepresentation in an application. Termination does not
extinguish amounts owed to the corporation.

2.11 **Member meetings.** The annual meeting is held each year at a time and place the board sets,
within 120 days after the end of the fiscal year. Special meetings may be called by the board, the
president or members holding at least 10% of the votes. Notice is given at least 10 and not more
than 90 days before the meeting, stating the date, time, place and matters to be voted on.

2.12 **Quorum and voting.** A quorum for a member meeting is 20% of members entitled to vote,
including at least five members. Each member has one vote. Except where the articles, these bylaws
or the law require a greater vote, action requires a majority of votes cast at a meeting with a
quorum. Members may vote in person, by written proxy or by written ballot. Directors are elected by
written ballot.

2.13 **Action without a meeting.** Members may act without a meeting by unanimous written consent
if the law permits, or by written ballot if the board authorizes it.

2.14 **Record date.** The board may fix a record date for notice, voting or any other purpose, not
more than 60 days before the action.

## Article 3 — Board of Directors

3.1 **Number and term.** The board consists of three to nine directors, with the exact number fixed
by board resolution. Directors serve two-year terms and may be staggered so that roughly half the
seats are elected each year. A director serves until a successor is elected or the director resigns
or is removed.

3.2 **Powers.** The board manages the corporation's business and affairs, including the budget,
dues, admissions, debt, land decisions, major contracts, insurance and operating rules, subject to
member approval where the articles, these bylaws or the law require it.

3.3 **Meetings.** The board meets at least quarterly, and more often if needed. Regular meetings
may be held on a schedule the board adopts. Special meetings may be called by the president or by
any two directors with at least 48 hours' notice. Directors may attend by telephone or video if all
can hear one another.

3.4 **Quorum and action.** A majority of the authorized directors is a quorum. Action requires a
majority of directors present at a meeting with a quorum. The board may act by unanimous written
consent.

3.5 **Vacancies and removal.** A vacancy may be filled by the board for the remainder of the term.
A director may be removed for cause by a majority of the board, or as the law permits, after notice
and an opportunity to respond.

3.6 **Compensation.** Directors serve without compensation for board service. Reasonable expenses
may be reimbursed under the financial controls policy. A director may be paid for separate services
only with board approval, disclosure and recusal.

3.7 **Committees.** The board may establish committees, including finance, governance, admissions
and facilities. A committee may act for the board only as the board resolves and as the law allows.
The board may not delegate decisions reserved to it by the articles, these bylaws or the law.

3.8 **Standard of care.** Each director shall act in good faith, with the care an ordinarily prudent
person in a like position would exercise, and in a manner the director reasonably believes to be in
the corporation's best interest. Directors may rely on reports from officers, counsel, accountants
and committees as the law permits.

3.9 **Conflict of interest.** Directors, officers and committee members shall follow the conflict of
interest policy adopted by the board, disclose interests, and recuse themselves from discussion and
voting on matters in which they have a material interest.

## Article 4 — Officers

4.1 **Officers.** The officers are a president, a vice president, a secretary and a treasurer. The
board may combine roles where the law allows and may appoint assistant officers.

4.2 **President.** The president presides at member and board meetings, signs documents the board
authorizes, and supervises the general manager's execution of board decisions.

4.3 **Vice president.** The vice president performs the president's duties when the president is
absent and performs other duties the board assigns.

4.4 **Secretary.** The secretary keeps minutes and the record book, gives notices, maintains the
membership ledger and certifies records. The secretary signs the certificate of adoption of these
bylaws.

4.5 **Treasurer.** The treasurer oversees accounts, budgets, reserve statements, tax filings and
financial reports, and presents a financial report at each board meeting.

4.6 **General manager.** The board may appoint a general manager, who is not a director, to run
day-to-day operations within the approved budget. The general manager reports to the board and has
the authority the board delegates in writing.

## Article 5 — Contracts, Checks and Corporate Actions

5.1 **Contracts.** The board may authorize any officer or agent to enter contracts on the
corporation's behalf. Authority must be in writing and within the approved budget.

5.2 **Checks and disbursements.** Checks and electronic payments require the approvals stated in
the financial controls policy. Two approvals are required above the threshold the board sets.

5.3 **Deposits and funds.** All funds are deposited in accounts the board designates. Collected
transient occupancy tax is held as a liability and remitted as required.

5.4 **Loans to directors and officers.** The corporation shall not make loans to directors or
officers. Any loan to the corporation requires board approval, written terms and disclosure.

5.5 **Debt, security interests and receivables.** The board may borrow for corporate purposes,
mortgage or deed of trust the corporation's real property, grant a security interest in the
corporation's payment intangibles — including dues, occupancy charges and rental-pool receipts — and
enter an assignment of receivables, lockbox or deposit account control agreement with a lender. The
authority must be exercised by board resolution stating the principal amount, the purpose, the
collateral and the repayment source, and the board must find that projected NOI covers debt service
at the coverage ratio the resolution states. Member approval is required where Article 10 or the law
requires it.

## Article 6 — Indemnification and Insurance

The corporation shall indemnify directors, officers and agents to the fullest extent permitted by
the California Nonprofit Corporation Law, and may advance expenses consistent with that law. The
corporation may purchase directors and officers liability, general liability, property, event and
employment practices insurance. Indemnification does not extend to matters that may not be
indemnified by law.

## Article 7 — Membership Ledger and Reports

The secretary maintains a ledger recording each member's name, contact details, tier, contribution,
dues status, admission date and termination date. The treasurer presents an annual financial report
to the members within 120 days after the fiscal year ends, showing revenue, expenses, reserves and
any owner-pool settlements. The board reports material legal, insurance or agency developments at
the next member meeting.

## Article 8 — Rental Pool

8.1 **Authority.** The board may operate a managed rental pool for members who release unused
space-nights. Participation is voluntary and requires a signed addendum.

8.2 **Split.** The board sets the rental split. The split in effect at adoption is 60% of lodging
receipts to the releasing member and 40% to the corporation, before agreed operating costs.

8.3 **Costs.** Direct booking and turnover, venue and parking costs, and incremental administration
are deducted as the board's policy states. The policy must be disclosed to participating members
before they release dates.

8.4 **Tax.** The corporation collects and remits transient occupancy tax on taxable stays as a
liability. Owner payouts are reported as the law and the corporation's CPA require.

8.5 **No guarantee.** The pool is not a promise of occupancy, income or a particular payout. The
board may suspend the pool for maintenance, agency conditions or insurance requirements.

## Article 9 — Rules and Occupancy

The board may adopt occupancy rules, vehicle rules, quiet hours, guest limits, event conditions,
water and waste rules, fire restrictions and parking rules. Rules must be consistent with the
articles, these bylaws, the approved use and the occupancy agreement. Members receive notice of
material rule changes.

## Article 10 — Amendments

10.1 **Board amendments.** The board may amend these bylaws by a majority vote at a meeting with a
quorum, except where the law, the articles or this article requires member approval.

10.2 **Member approval.** Amendments that materially affect member voting rights, capital
contributions, dues beyond the annual adjustment, transfer or refund terms, a mortgage or deed of
trust on the corporation's real property, dissolution, or the cooperative provisions require
approval by a majority of members voting at a meeting with a quorum.

10.3 **Notice.** Proposed amendments must be described in the meeting notice.

## Article 11 — Records and Inspection

A member may inspect the articles, bylaws, minutes of member meetings, the membership ledger
(subject to privacy redaction), and financial statements, for a proper purpose and on reasonable
notice. The board may require a written request stating the purpose. The corporation may withhold
records protected by law, privilege or privacy, and may charge the reasonable cost of copies.

## Article 12 — Dissolution

Dissolution requires the approval of the board and a majority of members voting at a meeting with a
quorum, and any other approval the law requires. After payment of debts, liabilities and the costs
of winding up, remaining assets are distributed as the articles provide, to members or to one or
more nonprofit or cooperative organizations approved by the members. No distribution may violate
the California Nonprofit Corporation Law or the terms of any grant, loan or agency approval.

## Article 13 — Construction and Severability

These bylaws are governed by California law. If any provision is invalid, the remaining provisions
continue in effect. Headings are for convenience only. References to statutes include successor
provisions.
