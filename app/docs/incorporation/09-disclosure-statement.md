# Disclosure Statement — Holons Coop

**For prospective members.** Read this before you pay anything.

> DRAFT — not an offer, not a solicitation and not legal advice. Counsel's DRE and securities
> analysis is not complete, and no public report has been issued. No membership may be offered or
> sold until that work is finished and the Co-op authorises the offering in writing.

## 1. What is being described

Holons Coop is a proposed California nonprofit mutual benefit corporation that intends to own and
operate a recreational vehicle community offering resident, seasonal and absent-owner memberships
on one parcel in Imperial County. The project is
a concept under study. It has no operating history, no approved park, no permit to operate and no
guaranteed water or wastewater service.

## 2. No assurance that the project will happen

The Co-op may not acquire the land, may not obtain county or health approvals, may not raise the
capital it needs, may build a different project, or may not open at all. Timelines in the study and
roadmap are illustrative and depend on agencies, studies, construction and funding.

## 3. Your capital is at risk

3.1 A membership contribution is capital for the project, not a deposit, not a loan and not an
investment with a promised return.

3.2 You may lose all or part of your contribution if the project fails, costs more than expected, or
cannot obtain approvals.

3.3 The contribution is not insured and is not held in trust unless the board and counsel require
it. It may be spent on approved project costs before the park is complete.

3.4 A refund or redemption is not guaranteed. Any redemption policy is subject to the Co-op's
reserves, debts, budget and California law, and may be limited, delayed or declined.

3.5 The Co-op may borrow against its receivables. If it does, dues and rental-pool receipts may be
assigned to a lender, paid into a controlled account and applied to debt service before other uses,
and the Co-op's ability to reduce dues or pay out reserves may be limited by the loan documents. A
member note, if you elect one, is unsecured debt of the Co-op, ranks behind secured debt, and is not
a share or an interest in land.

## 4. You own a share in the co-op, not a lot in the ground

A membership is a share in Holons Coop, the corporation that owns the parcel and the improvements
on it. The share is personal property, recorded in the membership ledger, and it is what makes you a
co-owner of the co-op alongside the other members. It is not a deed, not a recorded lot, not a
leasehold in a specific space and not an interest in the land. The occupancy agreement sets what
your occupancy type allows: a seasonal licence, absent-owner use with rental-pool participation, or
— where the approved use permits it — resident occupancy, which is a tenancy under California law.
Residential occupancy, manufactured homes and deeded lots each require their own approvals and
documents, and a seasonal member has no right of residency.

## 5. Approvals are not in place

No county zoning confirmation, conditional use permit, park permit, water approval, wastewater
approval, fire access approval, food-service approval or building permit has been issued for this
project. The study's reading of the zoning table is not a county determination. Health and fire
authorities may impose conditions that change the design, cost or capacity.

## 6. Costs, dues and assessments may rise

6.1 The project budget is an illustrative allowance, not a fixed-price contract. Construction,
utility, insurance and professional costs may exceed it.

6.2 Dues of $150 per month are an initial figure. The board may raise dues on notice, and members
may be asked to approve a capital call for an approved project.

6.3 The study's membership capital of $190,000 may not cover a park with the community buildings
shown in the concept drawings. A revised capital budget may be required before construction.

## 7. Rental income is not guaranteed

The rental pool is optional. The study's occupancy, nightly rate, season length and payout figures
are illustrative. The Co-op does not guarantee bookings, occupancy, revenue, a payout amount, or that
the pool will operate in any season. Transient occupancy tax is collected and remitted and is not
revenue.

## 8. Membership is illiquid

Membership is not freely transferable. A transfer requires board approval, a qualified transferee
and any transfer fee. There is no public market, and you may be unable to sell or recover your
contribution.

## 9. Governance

Members have one vote each. The board manages the corporation and may make decisions you disagree
with, including budgets, dues, admissions, project scope, debt and operating rules, subject to the
bylaws and member votes required by law. Directors and officers are volunteers and may have limited
experience with parks, construction or hospitality.

## 10. Insurance and liability

The Co-op intends to carry park, general liability, property, directors and officers, and event
insurance, but coverage may be unavailable, limited or expensive. Insurance does not protect your
contribution. You are responsible for insuring your own RV, vehicles and property.

## 11. Tax

The Co-op does not yet know its tax treatment or yours. Contributions, dues, rental receipts,
payouts and any patronage allocation may have tax consequences. Consult your own tax adviser. The
Co-op may be required to report payouts.

## 12. Securities and offering status

The Co-op has not determined whether membership interests are securities, has not filed a public
report, and has not registered or qualified any offering. Nothing in this statement or the business
plan is an offer to sell or a solicitation of an offer to buy. Any offering will be made only
through documents cleared by counsel and, where required, by the Commissioner of Financial
Protection and Innovation.

## 13. Environmental and site conditions

The parcel is in a desert environment with heat, dust, wind, flood and seismic exposure. Water
supply, wastewater disposal and fire access are unresolved until studies and agency approvals are
complete. The land may contain conditions that require mitigation.

## 14. Conflicts and related parties

Founders, directors or their affiliates may provide services, finance costs, or hold interests in
land or vendors. Interested transactions must be disclosed and approved under the conflict of
interest policy, with the interested person recusing.

## 15. Litigation and regulatory change

The Co-op may face disputes with agencies, neighbours, members, contractors or guests. Laws and
agency policies may change in ways that affect the project, its cost or its ability to operate.
