# Financial Controls and Dues Policy — Holons Coop

Adopted by the board of directors on [date].

> Draft for board adoption. The CPA should review the dues, reserve and owner-settlement sections
> before the first billing cycle.

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets how the corporation budgets, collects dues, holds reserves, approves spending,
handles transient occupancy tax and settles rental-pool payouts. It is written so that no single
person can move money without a record and a second check.

## 2. Budget

2.1 The treasurer prepares an annual budget before the start of each fiscal year, showing baseline
services, administration, upkeep, insurance, utilities, reserves, debt service and expected rental
income.

2.2 The board adopts the budget by resolution. Spending outside the approved budget by more than
$[____] or 10% of a line requires board approval.

2.3 The budget distinguishes baseline costs covered by dues from incremental rental costs covered by
rental receipts. No cost is counted twice.

## 3. Dues

3.1 Dues are $150 per member per month until changed by the board on at least 30 days' written
notice.

3.2 Dues are billed on the first of each month and are due on the 15th. Payment may be by ACH,
card or check as the treasurer provides.

3.3 A member who is 30 days late receives a written reminder. At 60 days late, the board may suspend
occupancy and rental-pool participation after notice. At 90 days late, the board may terminate
membership under the bylaws.

3.4 A hardship arrangement may be approved by the treasurer in writing, with the terms recorded and
reported to the board.

3.5 Dues are member income to the corporation, not rent and not a capital contribution. Dues are
recognised as revenue when billed, with an allowance for doubtful accounts.

## 4. Reserves

4.1 The corporation maintains an operating reserve equal to at least three months of baseline
operating costs.

4.2 The corporation maintains a capital reserve for replacement of water, wastewater, power,
buildings and access infrastructure. The board sets an annual target and reviews it with the budget.

4.3 The opening reserve of $10,000 in the project budget is held for opening costs and may not be
used for construction overruns without board approval.

4.4 Reserve funds are held in a separate account or a separately tracked ledger, and are reported at
each board meeting.

## 5. Approvals and disbursements

5.1 Two approvals are required for any payment above $1,000: the treasurer and one director, or the
president and the treasurer.

5.2 Payments above $[____] require prior board resolution, a written contract and a budget line.

5.3 No officer may sign a cheque to themselves, and no director or officer may approve a payment to
an entity in which the person has a material interest without disclosure and recusal.

5.4 Purchases are made from an approved vendor list where one exists. Three quotes are required
above $[____] unless the board records why that is impractical.

5.5 Petty cash is limited to $[____] and reconciled monthly.

## 6. Transient occupancy tax

6.1 The corporation registers with the Imperial County Tax Collector and collects the published 8%
TOT on taxable stays.

6.2 TOT is recorded as a liability, held in a designated ledger or account, and remitted on the
county's schedule. It is never counted as revenue, included in the rental split or used for
operating expenses.

6.3 A monthly reconciliation compares bookings, TOT collected and TOT remitted. Differences are
investigated and documented.

## 7. Rental-pool settlements

7.1 Lodging receipts are split 60% to the releasing member and 40% to the corporation, before
agreed direct costs.

7.2 Direct costs are charged at the rates in the rental-pool addendum: 25% of gross lodging for
booking and turnover, 35% of venue receipts, 20% of parking receipts, and $3,000 per year of
incremental administration.

7.3 Settlements are prepared monthly during the season, reviewed by the treasurer, and accompanied
by a statement showing nights, gross lodging, split, costs, TOT and net payout.

7.4 Payouts require the treasurer's approval and a second review above $1,000. Owner payment details
are kept confidential.

7.5 The CPA determines the reporting obligations for owner payouts, including information returns
where required.

## 8. Banking and reconciliation

8.1 The board designates the bank and the authorised signers. Signers are reviewed when officers
change.

8.2 Bank accounts are reconciled monthly. The reconciler may not be the person who writes the
cheques.

8.3 Online banking requires dual approval for transfers above $1,000 and alerts to the treasurer and
president.

8.4 The corporation does not make loans to directors or officers.

## 9. Reporting

9.1 The treasurer presents a monthly report to the board: cash balances, reserve balances, dues
collected and arrears, rental receipts and payouts, TOT collected and remitted, spending against
budget, and any variance over $[____].

9.2 The board receives a quarterly statement of reserves and a season-end report comparing actual
results with the budget and the study's scenarios.

9.3 Members receive an annual financial report within 120 days after the fiscal year ends.

## 11. Capital calls and debt

11.1 A capital call requires member approval as the bylaws provide, a stated purpose and a written
schedule.

11.2 Borrowing requires board approval, a written term sheet, a maturity and debt-service analysis,
and disclosure to members. The board must confirm that projected NOI covers debt service at the
agreed coverage ratio before signing.

## 12. Insurance and risk

The treasurer confirms that property, general liability, D&O and event cover are in force and that
premiums are paid before the season opens. A certificate of insurance is obtained from any vendor
working on site.

## 13. Review and records

The board reviews this policy annually. Financial records are kept under the records policy for at
least seven years. An independent review or audit may be commissioned by the board or required by a
lender or agency.
