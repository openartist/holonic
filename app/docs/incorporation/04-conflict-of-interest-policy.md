# Conflict of Interest Policy — Holons Coop

Adopted by the board of directors on [date].

> Draft for board adoption. Counsel should confirm the policy meets California nonprofit and
> cooperative requirements before it is relied on.

## 1. Purpose

This policy protects the corporation's decisions from being influenced by personal interests. It
sets out how directors, officers, committee members and the general manager disclose interests,
step out of decisions, and record what happened. It applies to the corporation's own decisions and
to decisions about the rental pool, contracts, admissions and land.

## 2. Who is covered

- Every director and officer.
- Every member of a board committee with decision-making authority.
- The general manager and any staff member who influences purchasing, admissions or settlements.
- Any agent acting for the corporation on a transaction.

## 3. What must be disclosed

A covered person must disclose any material interest, directly or through a family member or an
entity the person controls, in a matter before the corporation. Examples:

- A contract for construction, utilities, insurance, legal, accounting or management services.
- A sale, purchase, lease or option involving land or equipment.
- A membership admission, transfer or redemption involving the person or a relative.
- A rental-pool settlement or booking involving the person's own space.
- A loan, guarantee or capital contribution to or from the person.
- Outside employment or a business that competes with the corporation or supplies it.

A "family member" means a spouse, domestic partner, parent, child, sibling, grandparent,
grandchild, or anyone sharing the person's household.

## 4. Disclosure procedure

1. **Annual statement.** Each covered person signs the statement in Attachment A at adoption and
   each year, listing interests that could create a conflict.
2. **Transaction disclosure.** Before discussion of a specific matter, the person discloses the
   interest, its nature and its approximate value, and answers questions from the board.
3. **Recusal.** The person leaves the room for the discussion and vote, and does not attempt to
   influence other directors. The person may answer factual questions if the board asks, then
   withdraws again.
4. **Record.** The minutes state the interest disclosed, the recusal, the remaining directors who
   voted, and the board's finding that the transaction is fair and in the corporation's interest.
5. **Approval.** An interested transaction requires approval by a majority of disinterested
   directors, even if that is less than a quorum, and only after the board considers alternatives
   and the terms.

## 5. Standards for interested transactions

The board may approve an interested transaction only if it finds that:

- The transaction is fair and reasonable to the corporation at the time it is approved.
- The terms are no less favorable than those available from an unrelated party, or the board
  records why an exception is justified.
- The corporation can afford the commitment within the approved budget and reserves.
- The transaction is consistent with the articles, bylaws and applicable law.

## 6. Gifts and hospitality

Covered persons may not accept gifts, travel, entertainment, discounts or hospitality from a
supplier, contractor, applicant or member that could reasonably influence a decision. Nominal
items of insignificant value are permitted. Anything else must be disclosed and refused or
returned.

## 7. Confidentiality

A covered person may not use confidential information about the corporation, members, applicants or
the rental pool for personal benefit or disclose it except as authorized or required by law.

## 8. Outside interests and time

Covered persons may hold outside employment, offices and investments. They must disclose interests
that could conflict and must not use their position to divert an opportunity that belongs to the
corporation. A corporate opportunity must first be offered to the board.

## 9. Violations

If the board finds a violation, it may require the person to account for any benefit, rescind the
transaction where possible, remove the person from a committee, or take other action the law and
bylaws permit. A violation is not by itself grounds for termination of membership, but it may be
grounds for removal from the board.

## 10. Records and review

The secretary keeps signed statements and conflict minutes for at least seven years. The board
reviews this policy annually and after any material change in the corporation's activities.
